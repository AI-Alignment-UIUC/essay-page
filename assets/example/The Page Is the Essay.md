## Intro

Dear reader,

This page is a demo of the essay-page kit. Everything on it comes from one Markdown note, and each part of the layout shows up here once so you can see what it does. The first paragraph opens on a drop cap and a few words in capitals, the way a printed essay opens.

## Why one column

The essay is the focus. The text sits in one serif column in the middle of the window, with nothing beside it. An earlier version put a dashboard beside the column, and the essay felt boxed in. So the margins stay empty, and the room goes to what the essay itself holds: quotes and photographs that break out past the column.

> The dogmas of the quiet past are inadequate to the stormy present.
>
> Abraham Lincoln, Annual Message to Congress (1862)

A quote with no face beside it runs large under an orange mark, and its words light up one by one as you scroll. Body text is set at about 20 pixels on a laptop, with a line height of 1.62. A number the essay leans on, like 685 pixels, the width of the column on the AI 2027 site, gets a marker stroke the first time it comes up.

## Pictures that carry the point

People have gathered in public to claim a say in their own government for centuries. A strip shows a sequence like that all at once, side by side, oldest first.

A picture is shown whole, with no crop, blur or screen on it. Its caption says only what it is. Who made it goes in the Pictures roll at the end, which the licences of some photos require.

Abraham Lincoln gave his second inaugural address in March 1865, a month before the war ended. A wide picture breaks out of the column, but it is never taller than the window.

## The weight of knowing

A face beside a quote reads as the person who said it. So a spread puts the speaker's own portrait beside their words, and the sides alternate down the page.

> If atomic bombs are to be added as new weapons to the arsenals of a warring world, or to the arsenals of the nations preparing for war, then the time will come when mankind will curse the names of Los Alamos and Hiroshima.
>
> J. Robert Oppenheimer, accepting the Army-Navy Excellence Award (1945)

Some passages are heavier than the rest. When the reader reaches one, the lights go out, and that stretch is set in cream on black from edge to edge.

> We turned the switch, saw the flashes, watched them for about ten minutes, and then switched everything off and went home. That night I knew that the world was headed for sorrow.
>
> Leo Szilard, on the night of 3 March 1939

The dark band ends where the mood changes again, and the paper comes back.

## What you can do with it

1) Writing a letter? Draft it as a Markdown note, then run the sync script to turn it into the page's JSON.
2) Need a picture? Look for public domain or openly licensed work first, and keep the maker and the licence beside the picture in the config.
3) Want less motion? Add ?loud=0 to the address. Every animation on the page reads one dial, and reduced motion keeps only a short fade.
4) Ready to share? Keep the draft behind its switch, and send the unlisted copy until the essay is final.

## Close
